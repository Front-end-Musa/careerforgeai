# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Early-career job seekers who need to turn scattered experience, internships, school projects, and first professional roles into credible application materials. They are often applying under time pressure, comparing roles across many tabs, and trying to avoid mistakes that make them look less prepared than they are.

## Product Purpose

ResumeCrafts AI helps early-career applicants build ATS-friendly resumes, generate practical cover letters, organize applications, and move through a job search with less uncertainty. Success means users can create polished, truthful materials from their own details, understand what to improve next, and keep application work in one focused workspace.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, practical, focused. The product should feel like a capable career workspace: calm enough to reduce anxiety, direct enough to keep users moving, and credible enough that generated content feels reviewable rather than magical.

## Anti-references

This should not look or feel like a generic SaaS template, a flashy AI toy, a corporate HR portal, resume-mill spam, or an overcomplicated dashboard. Avoid decorative AI tropes, inflated claims, gimmicky automation language, and workflows that make early-career users feel evaluated before they feel helped.

## Design Principles

- Help before impressing: prioritize clear next actions, readable guidance, and useful defaults over spectacle.
- Make progress visible: show what is complete, what needs attention, and what the user can do next.
- Keep AI accountable: generated content should feel editable, grounded in the user's inputs, and easy to review.
- Reduce decision load: use simple choices, strong hierarchy, and focused screens for high-stakes job-search tasks.
- Earn trust through restraint: consistent controls, plain language, and accessible contrast matter more than novelty.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, and form feedback. Support reduced-motion preferences, avoid color-only status communication, and write labels and errors for users who may be new to job-search vocabulary.
